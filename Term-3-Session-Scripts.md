# Term 3 — *"One Thing I'd Fix"*
### 8 session scripts for Coach Dad to run with Emeka

🛠️ **Mode:** Respond (*fix the problem that's already here*) · **Pillar:** Community
**Academic targets baked in:** **reading for the main idea** (find the real point) · **opinion writing**
(claim + reasons: *"I think ___ because ___"*) · speaking & listening (a real interview) · neat mechanics.
**The real-world problem she's solving:** *something that's already bugging a real person outside the family* —
a neighbor, a classmate, a coach, the librarian.

> **What she builds:** a small, real fix for someone else's problem — a helper, a sign, a tiny info page, or
> a short **opinion piece** (a claim with reasons) — and she **gives it to the real person** who has the problem.

> **How Term 3 connects to Terms 1 & 2:** the first two expeditions were 🔭 **Foresight** — she caught problems
> *before* they happened, first in her own brain, then at home. Now the problem is **already here**, and it
> belongs to **someone else.** This is the **outward turn**: same detective eyes, pointed at her community.

---

## How to run these (read once)

- **Length:** each session works in **~30–45 min**. Consistency beats length.
- **The fixed rhythm (every session):**
  1. **Circle + Callback** (2 min) — one-word check-in, then *retrieve last time's thinking.*
  2. **Reading rep** (4 min) — read a short thing together and find the **main idea** (her reading target).
  3. **Opinion rep** (3 min, from Session 4 on) — one line: *"I think ___ because ___."* (her writing target).
  4. **The Move** (5 min) — today's named detective move.
  5. **Build / Think** (15–20 min) — she directs, you help.
  6. **Share + Log + Open Loop** (5 min) — she shows it, writes one neat log line, takes a question out the door.
- **The two rules that never bend:**
  - **Open with a Callback** (make her remember last time). **Close with an Open Loop** (a real question she carries out the door).
  - **She decides. You help.** Protect the seed — the idea and the fix are hers.
- **The outward-turn rules (new this term — say them to her):** we only help people who **want** help; we ask
  **"is it okay if I try to help with that?"** first; we use **first names only**; and a grown-up is **always with her.**
- **Kit:** her **"Emeka's Expedition Log"** notebook, pencil/markers, a way to **record an interview** (notes or a
  voice memo), and one device for the build in Session 5. A few short things to read (a note, a sign, a page).
- **Tone:** you're her **fix-it partner and her witness.** A stranger's problem isn't hers to *own* — it's hers to
  *help with*. The goal isn't a perfect fix; it's a real one, given to a real person.

> **Grown-up note on the two academic skills:** *Main idea* = "what is this really about?" (not every little detail
> — the big point). *Opinion writing* = a **claim** (*"I think we should…"*) backed by **reasons** (*"because…"*) and
> a bit of **evidence** (*"I know because…"*). Every session nudges one or both forward.

## SESSION 1 — NOTICE
**Loop step:** *A real problem someone already has.* · **The Move:** "Notice on Purpose" — *cue: "What bugs somebody?"*
🎯 **Objective:** she finds a **real problem that belongs to a real person** outside the family — no fixing yet, just noticing.

**Reading rep (4 min):** read a short sign or note together (a flyer, a rule posted at the park, a note home).
> "What's this **mostly** about — the main idea? Not every word, the **big point.** Tell me in one sentence."

**Build / Think:** in her log she starts a **"Problems I Notice" list** — things she sees other people struggle with.

**💬 Say this (slow, curious):**
> "The last two times, we caught problems *before* they happened. Today's different — we're looking for a problem
> that's **already here**, and it belongs to **somebody else.**"
> "Think about people you see a lot — a neighbor, a kid in your class, a coach, the librarian. What's something
> that seems **annoying or hard for them**? What do they sigh about?"
> "You're not fixing anything yet — just **noticing on purpose.** Let's list 3 things that bug somebody."

**🗣️ Share:** she reads her list and **circles the one** she cares about most.

**🚪 Open loop (out the door):**
> "This week, **watch that person** once when the problem happens. Don't fix it — just catch *exactly* what goes
> wrong. Bring it to me."

**✍️ Log line:** *"A real problem someone has is ___. The person is ___."*

---

## SESSION 2 — LISTEN
**Loop step:** *Real words from the person who has it.* · **The Move:** "Listen for the Real Words" — *cue: "Let them talk."*
🎯 **Objective:** she **interviews** the real person and brings back their **actual words** (not her guesses).

**🔁 Callback:**
> "Who is your person, and what did you catch going wrong this week? Tell me exactly what you saw."

**Reading rep (4 min):** read a short paragraph (a page from a book, a note). *"What's the main idea? Which sentence tells you the most?"*

**The Move + Build — the interview (do it together, with the grown-up):**
> "Engineers don't guess what people need — they **ask.** Let's make **3 questions** for your person. Real ones,
> like: *'What's the tricky part for you?'* … *'What do you wish was easier?'* … *'What have you already tried?'*"
> Then, with you there: she asks, and **writes down their real words** — quotes, not summaries.
> "Your job is to **listen more than you talk.** Catch their *exact* words. Those words are treasure."

**🗣️ Share:** she reads back **one real quote** the person actually said.

**🚪 Open loop:**
> "Look at your quotes tonight. Which word or line do they say with the **most feeling**? Star it."

**✍️ Log line:** *"The realest thing they said was: '___.'"*

---

## SESSION 3 — FIND THE GAP
**Loop step:** *The real problem under the obvious one.* · **The Move:** "Dig for the Real Problem" — *cue: "What's it really about?"*
🎯 **Objective:** she finds the **main idea of the problem** — the *real* gap hiding under the obvious complaint.

**🔁 Callback:**
> "Read me your starred quote. Say it the way they said it."

**Reading rep (4 min) — this IS the day's skill:** read her interview notes like a passage.
> "Finding the **main idea** of a story and finding the **real problem** are the *same move.* Look at all their
> words together — what are they **really** saying? Not the first complaint — the one **underneath.**"
> Example to model: *"'The line is too long' might really mean 'I don't know where to go, so I wait in the wrong line.'"*

**The Move + Think:**
> "Let's play **'the problem under the problem.'** They said the obvious thing — now, *why* is that a problem?
> …and why is *that* a problem? Keep asking 'why' until we hit the **real gap.**"
> "Write the real gap in one sentence: **'The real problem is that ___.'** That sentence is your target."

**🚪 Open loop:**
> "Watch your person one more time. Does the **real gap** you found match what actually happens? Bring me proof."

**✍️ Log line:** *"The obvious problem was ___. The REAL problem under it is ___."*

---

## SESSION 4 — SMALLEST THING
**Loop step:** *The first tiny version.* · **The Move:** "Smallest That Helps" — *cue: "First, not fancy."*
🎯 **Objective:** she makes the **smallest thing that could help** — and writes her **first opinion claim.**

**🔁 Callback:**
> "What's the real gap, in one sentence? Did watching prove it's the real one?"

**Reading rep (4 min):** a short read → *"Main idea in one sentence."*

**Opinion rep (3 min) — NEW, and it's writing practice:**
> "An **opinion** is what *you* think should happen. Say it as a **claim**: *'I think ___ would help.'* Now the
> magic word — **because.** *'I think ___ would help because ___.'* Write it neat: capital, period."

**The Move (brainstorm, then shrink):**
> "Let's brainstorm a **TON** of ways to help with the real gap — signs, a map, a helper card, a page, a checklist.
> Silly ones allowed, no judging. Go!" *(write 8–10)*
> "Now circle the **smallest one you could actually make and give** this week. Small enough to finish. Fancy is *later.*"

**🚪 Open loop:**
> "Sketch your smallest helper tonight. Ask yourself: *would this actually help THEM* — or just look nice?"

**✍️ Log line (NEAT — opinion, 2–3 sentences):** *"I think ___ would help because ___. I know because ___."*

---

## SESSION 5 — LIGHT IT UP *(the screen comes on)*
**Loop step:** *A live tool, given to a real person.* · **The Move:** "Give It Away" — *cue: "Real means you gave it."*
🎯 **Objective:** she **builds the real helper** and — the whole point — **gives it to the person who has the problem.**

**🔁 Callback:**
> "Show me your sketch. Who exactly is it for, and what gap does it fix?"

**Reading rep (4 min):** *"Main idea — one sentence." (keep the muscle warm.)*

**Build (15–20 min) — her project, you're the hands.** Make the real thing: a **sign / map / helper card / tiny info
page** — build it on the device (*she tells you what she wants, you type*) **or** by hand if that fits better. Keep it
**clear over pretty** — the reader has to *get it fast.* *Let her choose the words, the look, the name.*

**💬 The critique (the heart of it) — when the first version appears:**
> "You're the **boss** — I just make it. Read it like **your person** would. Do they get it in 5 seconds? What's confusing?"
> "What's **missing**? What would make them actually **use** it?"
> *(When she's stuck, don't rescue — ask: "What have you tried? What could you try next?" then wait.)*

**🗣️ Share + the real moment:** with you, she **gives it to the real person** (or plans exactly how/when she will).
> "Giving it away is what makes it **real.** A fix nobody gets isn't a fix yet."

**🚪 Open loop:**
> "When they use it — **watch their face and listen.** Bring me the **first thing they say.**"

**✍️ Log line:** *"I built ___ and gave it to ___."*

---

## SESSION 6 — IMPROVE
**Loop step:** *A better v2 + proof it helped.* · **The Move:** "Make It Better" — *cue: "What did they say?"*
🎯 **Objective:** she makes a **v2 from real feedback** and writes a real **opinion piece** (claim + two reasons).

**🔁 Callback:**
> "What did your person say or do when they used it? Their real words — did it help?"

**Reading rep (4 min):** read their feedback like a passage. *"What's the **main idea** of what they told you?"*

**Opinion rep → grows into the writing build:** today the opinion is the **proof.**
> "Write it like a real opinion piece: **claim + two reasons + evidence.** *'I think my helper worked because ___,
> and because ___. I know it helped because ___ (what they said or did).'* Neat — capitals, periods."

**The Move (improve from real use):**
> "The best builders change their thing after **real people** use it. From what they said — what's **one fix** that
> would make it work better? Make **v2.**"

**🚪 Open loop:**
> "Give them **v2.** Bring me whether it worked **better** than v1 — and *how you know.*"

**✍️ Log line (NEAT — the opinion piece):** *"I think my fix helped because ___ and ___. I know because ___."*

---

## SESSION 7 — SECOND STAR
**Loop step:** *A new or deeper build.* · **The Move:** "Reach Higher" — *cue: "Who else could this help?"*
🎯 **Objective:** she goes **deeper or wider** — makes the fix help **more people**, or solves the *next* layer.

**🔁 Callback:**
> "Did v2 work better than v1? How do you *know*? Say the proof."

**Reading rep (4 min):** a short read → main idea, plus one **inference**: *"What does the writer think? Show me the words."*

**The Move (reach higher):**
> "You fixed it for **one** person. **Who else** has this same problem? How could your fix help **them** too?"
> "Or go **deeper**: now that the first gap is fixed, what's the **next** gap you can see? Pick your **second star.**"

**🚪 Open loop:**
> "**Teach someone else** how your fix works, so it can keep helping without you. Bring me what they asked you."
> *(Teaching it is the deepest test.)*

**✍️ Log line:** *"My fix could also help ___. My second star is ___."*

---

## SESSION 8 — DEMO DAY
**Loop step:** *Show what you built and what you learned.* · **The Move:** "Name Your Smartest Thought" — *cue: "Name your smartest thought."*
🎯 **Objective:** she presents her fix + the proof it helped, and names her own smartest thinking.

**🔁 Callback — walk the whole journey together:**
> "Let's tell the whole story: you **Noticed** a real problem → **Listened** to the person → **dug for the real gap** →
> made the **smallest helper** → **gave it away** → **improved it** from real use → and **reached higher.** Wow."

**Reading + opinion = the celebration (5 min):** she reads her **opinion piece** out loud — claim, reasons, proof.
> "Listen to that — a real claim, real reasons, real evidence. That's a writer *and* a fixer."

**The Demo (to family + the person if they'll come):**
> "Show everyone your fix. Tell them: **whose** problem was it, what the **real gap** was, and **one real thing** the
> person said when it helped."

**The Move (metacognition — the real prize):**
> "Last question, the most important one: **the smartest thing I *thought* during this was…?**"
> *(thought — not did. Help her name a thinking move: "I found the real problem under the obvious one," "I listened
> instead of guessing," "I changed my fix after real people used it.")*

**🎉 Close:** celebrate big — a star on the wall, a certificate, her choice. She found a real person's real problem
and **helped** — and she can prove it. **That star is hers.**

**🚪 Open loop → into Term 4 (WORLD):**
> "You've fixed a problem in your **own brain**, in your **home**, and now in your **community.** Next time we point
> this brain at the **whole world** — something bigger you *wonder* about. Start noticing: what's one thing out in the
> world you really **want to understand**?"

**✍️ Log line:** *"The smartest thing I thought was ___."*
