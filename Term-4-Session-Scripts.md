# Term 4 — *"Something I Wonder About"*
### 8 session scripts for Coach Dad to run with Emeka — **the capstone**

🛠️ **Mode:** Respond (*capstone*) · **Pillar:** World
**Academic targets baked in:** **research reading** (read two sources, compare them, find the main idea across
both) · **narrative writing** (a story/explainer with a beginning, middle, end, and dialogue) · and the capstone
move — **naming her own thinking** (was this Foresight or Respond?).
**The real-world thing she's exploring:** *something she genuinely wonders about in the whole wide world.*

> **What she builds:** a little **story or explainer** that **teaches someone else** how to understand the thing
> she wondered about — and at the end she **names whether her project was Foresight or Respond, and why.**

> **How Term 4 closes the year:** she has now pointed her detective brain at **herself** (Term 1), her **home**
> (Term 2), and her **community** (Term 3). The last turn is the biggest — the **whole world.** And she's learned
> both superpowers; this term she **names** which one she used. That naming *is* the proof she owns both.

---

## How to run these (read once)

- **Length:** each session works in **~30–45 min**. Consistency beats length. This is the capstone — let it breathe.
- **The fixed rhythm (every session):**
  1. **Circle + Callback** (2 min) — one-word check-in, then *retrieve last time's thinking.*
  2. **Reading rep** (4 min) — read a short real thing and find the **main idea** (her research-reading target).
  3. **Writing rep** (3 min, from Session 4 on) — a line or two of her **story**, told out loud then written.
  4. **The Move** (5 min) — today's named move.
  5. **Build / Think** (15–20 min) — she directs, you help.
  6. **Share + Log + Open Loop** (5 min) — she shows it, writes one neat log line, takes a question out the door.
- **The two rules that never bend:**
  - **Open with a Callback.** **Close with an Open Loop.**
  - **She decides. You help.** This is *her* wonder, *her* story. Protect the seed.
- **Kit:** her **"Emeka's Expedition Log,"** pencil/markers, **two things to read** about her topic (a kid book, a
  short article, a video with captions, a museum page — a grown-up helps her find safe ones), and a device for Session 5.
- **Tone:** you're her **fellow explorer.** The world is huge and she gets to pick one true thing in it and become the
  person who can **explain it to someone else.** Teaching it is the whole point — you only really know a thing when
  you can help another person understand it.

> **Grown-up note on the two academic skills:** *Research reading* = reading **more than one source** and asking
> "do they agree? what's the **big idea** across both?" *Narrative writing* = telling it as a **story** with a
> beginning, a middle, and an end — and letting characters **talk** (quotation marks). The capstone adds one more:
> she **names her own thinking** — Foresight (*I got ready before the problem*) or Respond (*I fixed what was here*).

## SESSION 1 — WONDER
**Loop step:** *A real thing in the world she wonders about.* · **The Move:** "Wonder Out Loud" — *cue: "What do you really want to know?"*
🎯 **Objective:** she picks **one true thing in the world** she genuinely wonders about — big enough to matter, small enough to explore.

**Reading rep (4 min):** read a few sentences of nonfiction (an animal fact, a space fact, a how-things-work page).
> "What's the **main idea** — the one big thing this is telling us? Say it in a sentence."

**Build / Think:** in her log she makes a **"Wonder List"** — real questions about the world she'd love the answer to.

**💬 Say this (slow, curious):**
> "This is the last expedition of the whole year — and it's the biggest. We're pointing your brain at the **whole world.**"
> "What's something out there you **really wonder about**? Why the sky is blue? How bridges hold up cars? Where thunder
> comes from? Why some animals glow? There's no wrong wonder."
> "Let's list **5 wonders.** Then circle the **one** you'd most want to become an **expert** on — the one you'd love to
> *teach* someone."

**🗣️ Share:** she reads her Wonder List and **circles her one big wonder.**

**🚪 Open loop (out the door):**
> "This week, notice your wonder **out in the real world** — once. Where does it show up? Bring me what you saw."

**✍️ Log line:** *"The thing I wonder about is ___. I picked it because ___."*

---

## SESSION 2 — TWO SOURCES
**Loop step:** *Read to find out — from more than one place.* · **The Move:** "Read to Find Out" — *cue: "Ask the page."*
🎯 **Objective:** she does **real research** — reads **two** sources about her wonder and catches what each one says.

**🔁 Callback:**
> "What's your big wonder? Where did you notice it in the real world this week?"

**Reading rep = the day's work:** with you, she finds **two safe sources** (a book, an article, a captioned video).
> "Real researchers never trust just **one** page — they read **two or more.** Let's read **source one.** What's the
> **main idea**? Write one sentence. … Now **source two.** Main idea in one sentence."
> "Catch **two facts** from each source that help answer your wonder. Write them in your log."

**🗣️ Share:** she reads **one fact from each source.**

**🚪 Open loop:**
> "Look at your two sources tonight. Do they **agree**? Did one say something the other didn't? Star anything surprising."

**✍️ Log line:** *"Source 1 says ___. Source 2 says ___."*

---

## SESSION 3 — FIND THE HEART
**Loop step:** *The main idea across both sources.* · **The Move:** "What's the Real Story?" — *cue: "Say it in one line."*
🎯 **Objective:** she finds the **big idea across both sources** — the heart of what she'll teach.

**🔁 Callback:**
> "Read me your two main-idea sentences. Did your sources agree?"

**Reading rep (4 min) — comparing two texts:**
> "Put both sources side by side. What's the **big idea they BOTH tell you**? That shared big idea is the **heart** of
> your story."
> "Did they **disagree** anywhere? That's interesting too — a real explainer can say *'some people think this, others think that.'*"

**The Move + Think:**
> "Now boil it all down: if you had to teach your wonder in **one sentence**, what would it be? That's your **heart line.**"
> "Write it big: **'The big thing I want to teach is ___.'** Everything in your story will point at *that.*"

**🚪 Open loop:**
> "Tell **one person** your heart line. Did they understand it? Did they ask a question? Bring me their question."

**✍️ Log line:** *"The heart of what I learned is ___."*

---

## SESSION 4 — SHAPE THE STORY
**Loop step:** *Give it a beginning, middle, and end.* · **The Move:** "Give It a Shape" — *cue: "Beginning, middle, end."*
🎯 **Objective:** she **plans her story/explainer** — who it's for and how it goes from start to finish.

**🔁 Callback:**
> "What's your heart line? What did the person you told ask you?"

**Reading rep (4 min):** read a very short story or explainer. *"Where's the **beginning**, the **middle**, the **end**? How did the writer start so you wanted to keep reading?"*

**Writing rep (3 min) — NEW:** she says her **first sentence** out loud, then writes it neat.
> "A story pulls you in from the **first line.** Say yours out loud until it sounds exciting — *then* write it, capital and period."

**The Move (plan the shape):**
> "Every good story or explainer has a **shape**: a **beginning** (grab them), a **middle** (teach the heart), an **end**
> (a thought to keep). Let's plan yours in **three boxes.**"
> "**Who is this for?** A younger kid? A friend? Grandma? Picture *that* person the whole time you write."

**🚪 Open loop:**
> "Fill in your three boxes tonight — beginning, middle, end. Which box is hardest? Bring me that one."

**✍️ Log line (NEAT):** *"My story is for ___. Beginning: ___. Middle: ___. End: ___."*

---

## SESSION 5 — TELL IT *(the screen comes on)*
**Loop step:** *Make it come alive.* · **The Move:** "Make It Come Alive" — *cue: "Let them talk."*
🎯 **Objective:** she **writes the story/explainer** — with a real voice and a bit of **dialogue.**

**🔁 Callback:**
> "Which box was hardest? Let's start there together."

**Reading rep (4 min):** read a page **with dialogue.** *"See the quotation marks? Those show someone is **talking.** How does the talking make the story feel more alive?"*

**Writing rep → the build:** today the writing **is** the build.
> "Turn your three boxes into a real story or explainer. Write it on the device — **you say it, I type** — or by hand if
> that's better. Add a line of **dialogue**: someone **talking** with quotation marks. *'Wow,' said Coco, 'I never knew that!'*"

**💬 The critique (the heart of it) — when the first draft appears:**
> "You're the **author** — I'm just the keyboard. Read it out loud. Does the **beginning** grab you? Does the **middle**
> teach the heart? Does the **end** leave a thought?"
> "What's **missing**? Where could someone **talk**? Would your reader **understand** it?"
> *(When she's stuck, don't rescue — ask: "What have you tried? What could you try next?" then wait.)*

**🗣️ Share:** she **reads her story out loud** to the family.

**🚪 Open loop:**
> "Read it to yourself once more tonight. Find **one word** you could make stronger. Bring me the before and after."

**✍️ Log line:** *"I wrote a ___ about ___. My favorite line is ___."*

---

## SESSION 6 — TEACH IT
**Loop step:** *Teach someone else with it.* · **The Move:** "Teach to Know" — *cue: "If they get it, you got it."*
🎯 **Objective:** she **teaches a real person** her wonder using her story — the deepest test of understanding.

**🔁 Callback:**
> "What word did you make stronger? Read me the new line."

**Reading rep (4 min):** *"Main idea — one sentence." (keep the muscle warm.)*

**The Move + Build — teach it for real:**
> "You only *really* know something when you can **teach it** so someone else gets it. Pick your person — a younger kid,
> a friend, Grandma — and **teach them your wonder** using your story."
> "Watch their face. When did they go *'ohhh, I get it'*? When did they look confused? Their confusion tells you what to fix."

**🗣️ Share:** she tells you **the moment they understood** — and **one question** they asked.

**🚪 Open loop:**
> "Fix **one thing** in your story from teaching it — the confusing part. Bring me what you changed and why."

**✍️ Log line:** *"I taught ___ to ___. They finally got it when ___."*

---

## SESSION 7 — NAME YOUR POWER
**Loop step:** *Which superpower was this?* · **The Move:** "Name Your Power" — *cue: "Foresight or Respond?"*
🎯 **Objective:** the capstone move — she looks at all **four** expeditions and **names her thinking.**

**🔁 Callback:**
> "What did you fix after teaching it? Did the confusing part get clearer?"

**Reading rep (4 min):** read back her **own log lines** from the year like a book. *"What's the **main idea** of YOUR year?"*

**The Move (the capstone naming):**
> "You've learned **two superpowers** this year. **Foresight** — *catch the problem before it happens.* **Respond** —
> *fix the problem that's already here.* Let's look at all **four** of your builds."
> "Your **Smoke Detector** and your **Fair-Share Fixer** — were those Foresight or Respond? … Your **One Thing I'd Fix**
> and this **wonder story** — which were those?"
> "Now the big one: **which superpower did YOU use most?** Which one feels more like *you*? There's no wrong answer —
> naming it is the whole prize."

**🚪 Open loop:**
> "Watch the world this week for **one place** you could use your favorite superpower next. Bring it to Demo Day."

**✍️ Log line:** *"My four builds used ___ and ___. The superpower that feels most like me is ___ because ___."*

---

## SESSION 8 — DEMO DAY *(the year's finale)*
**Loop step:** *Show the whole year and name your smartest thought.* · **The Move:** "The Author's Star" — *cue: "Name your smartest thought."*
🎯 **Objective:** she presents her wonder story **and** the whole year — four builds, both superpowers — and names her own smartest thinking.

**🔁 Callback — walk the WHOLE YEAR together:**
> "Let's tell the whole story of your year: you built a **Smoke Detector** for your own facts → a **Fair-Share Fixer**
> for home → **One Thing I'd Fix** for your community → and a **wonder story** for the world. Four real builds. Wow."

**Reading + writing = the celebration (5 min):** she **reads her wonder story out loud** — the finished thing.
> "Listen to that — a real explainer, from real research, in your own voice. You're an **author** and a **researcher.**"

**The Demo (to family — make it big):**
> "Show everyone your **wonder story.** Tell them: what you **wondered**, what you **found** in your two sources, and
> **who you taught** it to. Then tell them your **favorite of the four builds** this year — and why."

**The Move (metacognition — the real prize of the whole year):**
> "The most important question of the entire year: **the smartest thing I *thought* this year was…?**"
> *(thought — not did. Help her name a big thinking move: "I learned to catch problems before they happen," "I found the
> real problem under the obvious one," "I read two sources instead of trusting one," "I can teach what I learn.")*

**🎉 Close — the Author's Star:** celebrate **huge.** She finished a **whole year** as the **author of her own learning** —
four real builds, both superpowers, given to real people. A star on the wall, a certificate, a special night — her choice.
**She didn't just do 3rd grade. She learned to keep thinking, and to face outward. That star doesn't go out.**

**🚪 Open loop → into next year:**
> "You know both superpowers now. Next year, the problems get bigger and so do you. Keep your Expedition Log close —
> and keep asking: *what's one thing near me I could make better?*"

**✍️ Log line:** *"The smartest thing I thought this year was ___."*
