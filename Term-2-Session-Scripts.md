# Term 2 — *"Before-It's-a-Fight Fair Shares"*
### 8 session scripts for Coach Dad to run with Emeka

🔭 **Mode:** Foresight (*catch the problem before it breaks*) · **Pillar:** Home
**Academic targets baked in:** **fair shares & fractions** (halves, thirds, fourths; equal shares) ·
explaining her thinking out loud · **retelling** what she reads (characters, setting, problem/solution).
**The real-world problem she's getting ahead of:** *an unfair split at home turning into a fight —
snacks, screen time, chores, the front seat.*

> **What she builds:** her own **"Fair-Share Fixer"** — a simple helper that splits things *equally*
> (into halves, thirds, fourths) and settles "that's not fair!" *before* it becomes a real argument.
> She's catching the **smoke** (the face someone makes right before the fight) and stopping the **fire.**

> **How Term 2 connects to Term 1:** last time she caught a problem inside *her own brain* (wobbly math
> facts). This time she points the **same detective eyes outward — at home.** Same loop, bigger world.

---

## How to run these (read once)

- **Length:** each session works in **~30–45 min**. Short and consistent beats long.
- **The fixed rhythm (every session):**
  1. **Circle + Callback** (2 min) — one-word check-in, then *retrieve last time's thinking.*
  2. **Fraction reps** (5 min) — the day's fair-share practice (real food/objects, then draw it).
  3. **Retell moment** (3 min) — she **retells** a short bit we read (this is her reading target).
  4. **The Move** (5 min) — today's named detective move.
  5. **Build / Think** (15–20 min) — she directs, you help.
  6. **Share + Log + Open Loop** (5 min) — she shows it, writes one neat log line, takes a question out the door.
- **The two rules that never bend:**
  - **Open with a Callback** (make her remember last time). **Close with an Open Loop** (a real question she carries out the door).
  - **She decides. You help.** Protect the seed — the ideas are hers.
- **Kit:** her **"Emeka's Expedition Log"** notebook, pencil/markers, and — the fun part — **real things to
  split**: a granola bar, crackers, a pizza night, a bag of grapes, LEGO, minutes on a timer. *(Most of
  this is hands-on and off-screen — the device only really comes on in Session 6.)*
- **The reading thread:** keep **one short story about fairness/sharing** going across the term (a picture
  book, a chapter, even a folktale you tell). Each session she **retells** a little of it — that *is* the
  reading practice. Suggested vibe: any "someone got more, then they made it fair" story.
- **Tone:** you're her **fairness detective partner.** Nobody's the bad guy. Unfair splits aren't
  someone being mean — they're *clues* that a fair-share tool is missing.

> **Grown-up note on fractions:** keep it concrete. A fraction is just **equal shares of one whole.**
> Half = 2 equal parts (1/2). Thirds = 3 equal parts (1/3). Fourths = 4 equal parts (1/4). The magic
> word is **EQUAL** — if the parts aren't equal, it isn't really halves/fourths. Let her *cut, pour, and
> deal out* real things first; the drawing and the words come after the hands.

## SESSION 1 — MAP IT
**Loop step:** *How does sharing normally work at home?* · **The Move:** "Map It" — *cue: "Draw how it works."*
🎯 **Objective:** she looks at how her family *already* splits things — no fixing yet, just seeing the machine.

**Fraction reps (5 min):** hand her **one** real thing (a granola bar / a cookie / a piece of paper).
> "Make this **fair for two people.** Show me." *(She halves it.)* "You just made **one half** each —
> that's **1/2.** Now make it fair for **four.** … Those are **fourths — 1/4** each. The secret rule is
> the parts have to be **EQUAL.** Are yours equal?"

**Retell moment (3 min):** read 2–3 pages of your fairness story. *"Retell it back to me — who's in it,
where are they, and what's the problem so far?"*

**Build / Think:** in her log she **draws the "sharing machine" at home** — *when we split something
(snack, screen time, the good seat), how does it happen right now? Who decides? Is it usually equal?*

**💬 Say this (slow, curious):**
> "Today we're fairness detectives — we're not fixing anything yet, just looking. Deal?"
> "Think about the last time you and someone **split** something at home. What was it? Who got more?"
> "How does your family usually decide who gets what — first grab? Biggest kid? A grown-up decides? Draw me the machine."
> "Where in your drawing does it usually feel **fair**? Where does it feel **unfair**? Mark those spots."

**🗣️ Share:** she shows her drawing and names **one fair split** and **one that often feels unfair.**

**🚪 Open loop (out the door):**
> "This week, catch **one time** at home when something gets split. Notice: was it **equal** or not?
> Remember exactly what it was — bring it to me."

**✍️ Log line:** *"At home, sharing feels fair when ___ and unfair when ___."*

## SESSION 2 — FIND THE WEAK SPOT
**Loop step:** *Which splits could start a fight?* · **The Move:** "Find the Weak Spot" — *cue: "Where does it wobble?"*
🎯 **Objective:** find the "weak spots" — the exact splits that tend to go wrong — *before* they blow up.

**🔁 Callback:**
> "Last time you were going to catch one split at home. What was it — equal or not? Tell me exactly what happened."

**Fraction reps (5 min):** the **Fair-Share Sort** with a small pile (grapes, crackers, LEGO).
> "Deal these out fair for **3** people. … Those are **thirds.** Now for **4.** Now here's a tricky one:
> **5 grapes for 2 people** — uh oh. Can you split the leftover one? *That's* where fights start."

**Retell moment (3 min):** next bit of the story. *"Retell me what's new — did the problem get better or worse?"*

**Build / Think — the Weak-Spot Hunt:**
> "Engineers find the weak spot *before* the bridge falls. Let's list every place at home where splitting
> gets **wobbly** — screen time, snacks, chores, the front seat, who's first. I'll write, you talk."
> Then look together: "Circle the **top 2** that most often turn into *'that's not fair!'* Those are our targets."

**🚪 Open loop:**
> "Pick the **#1 wobbliest split** in your house — that's our target. Watch it happen once this week without saying anything yet. Just spy."

**✍️ Log line:** *"The splits that wobble at home are ___. My #1 target is ___."*

---

## SESSION 3 — SMOKE BEFORE FIRE
**Loop step:** *What's the early signal?* · **The Move:** "Smoke Before Fire" — *cue: "Smell the smoke."*
🎯 **Objective:** name the "smoke" — the warning sign that an unfair split is *about* to become a fight.

**🔁 Callback:**
> "What was your #1 target split? When you spied on it, what did you see?"

**Fraction reps (5 min):** target split, made fair. *"Show me the fair version. What fraction does each person get — halves? thirds? fourths?"*

**Retell moment (3 min):** story. *"Retell the part where someone felt it was unfair. What did their face or voice do?"* *(links story feelings → real smoke signals)*

**The Move + Think:**
> "A smoke detector **beeps before the fire.** So — what's the **'smoke'** right before a fair-share fight?
> What does a person's **face** do? Their **voice**? Their **body**?"
> "Does someone go **quiet**? Cross their **arms**? Say *'how come YOU get—'*? That's the smoke."
> "Cool — when you see THAT, your detector should beep: *'fix the split now, before it's a fire!'*"

**🗣️ Share:** she **acts out** the smoke signal (the pre-fight face). *(Make it funny — she'll remember it.)*

**🚪 Open loop:**
> "Watch for the smoke this week — at home, at school, anywhere someone's about to feel cheated.
> **Count how many times** you see it."

**✍️ Log line:** *"The smoke before a fair-share fight is ___. When I see it, it means ___."*

---

## SESSION 4 — PLAY THE TAPE FORWARD
**Loop step:** *If nobody fixes the unfair split, then what?* · **The Move:** "Play the Tape Forward" — *cue: "Then what? Then what?"*
🎯 **Objective:** she *feels* why this matters (the "why" that powers the whole build).

**🔁 Callback:**
> "How many times did you see the smoke this week? What was happening each time?"

**Fraction reps (5 min):** a fair-vs-unfair face-off. *"Here's 8 crackers for 2. Unfair way: I take 6, you take 2. Fair way: ___ each. What fraction is fair? Which feels right?"*

**Retell moment (3 min):** story — near the end. *"Retell how they're trying to make it fair. Did it work?"*

**The Move:**
> "Let's play the tape forward like a movie. Someone gets an unfair split and nobody fixes it…
> then what? … and then what? … does it happen *again* tomorrow? … how does everyone feel by dinner?"
> "Now **rewind and play the *other* movie.** Someone catches the smoke and makes it a **fair share**…
> then what? … then how does the house feel?"
> "**Which movie do you want for your home?** … Good. That's why we're building the Fixer."

**🚪 Open loop:**
> "Tell **one person at home** which unfair split you're going to fix, and *why it matters to you.*"

**✍️ Log line (NEAT — this one's writing practice, 3–4 sentences, capitals + periods):**
> *"There are two movies. If nobody fixes the split, ___. If someone makes it fair, ___. I want the second movie because ___."*

## SESSION 5 — SMALLEST EARLY STOP
**Loop step:** *The tiniest thing that keeps it fair.* · **The Move:** "Smallest That Works" — *cue: "First, not perfect."*
🎯 **Objective:** design the smallest, do-it-every-time rule that stops an unfair split from becoming a fight.

**🔁 Callback:**
> "Who did you tell? What did they say when you told them why fair shares matter to you?"

**Fraction reps (5 min):** the classic fairness trick — **"you cut, I choose."** *"If you cut the bar
and I get to pick my piece first, how careful are you about making it EXACTLY half? Why does that make it fair?"*

**Retell moment (3 min):** finish the story. *"Retell the whole thing fast — beginning, problem, how it got fair. What's the **lesson**?"*

**The Move (brainstorm, then shrink):**
> "Let's brainstorm a **TON** of fair-share rules — silly ones allowed, no judging. Go!"
> *(write every idea — "you cut I choose," "take turns picking," "split it in equal parts first, then deal," aim for 8–10)*
> "Now circle the **smallest** rule you'd *actually* use every time. Small enough nobody can argue with it.
> Like: *'Before anyone grabs, we split it into equal parts first.'*"

**🚪 Open loop:**
> "Try your tiny fair-share rule **one time** before next session. Did it work, or was it too clunky?"

**✍️ Log line:** *"My smallest fair-share rule is ___."*

---

## SESSION 6 — BUILD THE STOP *(the screen comes on)*
**Loop step:** *Make the Fair-Share Fixer real.* · **The Move:** "Real Beats Perfect" — *cue: "It only counts when it's real."*
🎯 **Objective:** build the actual **Fair-Share Fixer** — *she directs, you drive.*

**🔁 Callback:**
> "Did your tiny rule work when you tried it? What got in the way?"

**Fraction reps (5 min):** the numbers her Fixer needs. *"If we split for 2, each gets 1/2. For 3? For 4?
What if there's a leftover — what do we do with it?"* *(These become the buttons/rows on her tool.)*

**Build (15–20 min) — her project, you're the hands.** Make a **Fair-Share Fixer**. Two good options —
**she picks**:
- **On the device (build it with AI — she tells you what she wants, you type):** a little page where you
  enter *how many people* and it shows the **fair share** (halves/thirds/fourths) + a "leftover rule."
- **On paper (a poster/wheel she designs):** a **Fair-Share Chart** — pictures of 1/2, 1/3, 1/4, and the
  house rule ("split equal first, then pick"). *(Great for kids who love the art side — that's her strength.)*

*Let her pick the name, colors, and look — ownership is the whole point.*

**💬 The critique (the heart of it) — when the first version appears:**
> "You're the **boss** of this build — I just type and cut. What should it show? What happens with a leftover?"
> "Do you want to **change** anything? What's **missing**? Would this actually stop a fight in YOUR house?"
> *(When she's stuck, don't rescue — ask: "What have you tried? What's one thing you could try next?" then wait.)*

**🗣️ Share:** she shows the family her **live Fair-Share Fixer** and who it's for (the whole house!).

**🚪 Open loop:**
> "Use your Fixer **the next time something gets split this week.** Bring me what happened — did it keep the peace?"

**✍️ Log line:** *"I built ___. It makes fair shares by ___."*

---

## SESSION 7 — BREAK YOUR OWN FIX
**Loop step:** *Try to make it unfair, then patch it.* · **The Move:** "Break Your Own Fix" — *cue: "Be your own villain."*
🎯 **Objective:** make the Fixer tougher from real use — *and find proof it kept the peace.*

**🔁 Callback:**
> "Did you use your Fixer this week? … Now the big question: did a fight that *usually* happens **not** happen?"

**Fraction reps = the PROOF step (5 min):** the trickiest split her Fixer handles.
> "Give me the hardest one — **7 cookies for 3 people.** Can your Fixer make that fair? What about the leftover cookie?"
> *(Great real fractions thinking: 2 each with 1 left → cut it in thirds, or the 'you cut I choose' rule.)*

**Retell moment (3 min):** *"Retell me a real moment this week where a split could've been a fight but wasn't. You're telling a true story now."*

**The Move (stress-test):**
> "Let's be **sneaky.** Try to make your Fixer **FAIL.** What split would break it? A weird number? Something
> that *can't* be cut equal (like the last turn on a game)? When would someone still yell 'not fair'?"
> "What's **one fix** so even sneaky-you can't break it?"

**🚪 Open loop:**
> "**Teach someone at home** how the Fixer works, so they can use it too. Bring me what they asked you." *(Teaching it is the deepest test.)*

**✍️ Log line (NEAT — 3–4 sentences, explaining):**
> *"My Fixer almost broke because ___. I fixed it by ___. I know it works because ___."*

---

## SESSION 8 — DEMO DAY
**Loop step:** *Show the fight that never happened.* · **The Move:** "Catch Yourself Thinking" — *cue: "Name your smartest thought."*
🎯 **Objective:** she presents her Fair-Share Fixer + the argument it prevented, and names her own smartest thinking.

**🔁 Callback — walk the whole journey together:**
> "Let's tell the whole story: you **Mapped** how your home shares → found the **weak spots** → named the
> **smoke** → played the **tape** → picked the **smallest rule** → **built** the Fixer → and **broke-and-fixed** it. Wow."

**Fraction reps = the celebration (5 min):** a lightning round of fair shares.
> "Split for 2 — what fraction? For 4? For 3 with a leftover? Look how fast and fair you are now." *(Cheer before → after.)*

**The Demo (to family):**
> "Show everyone your **Fair-Share Fixer.** Tell them: what fight does it catch *before* it starts?
> Then tell the true story of **one time this week it kept the peace.**"

**The Move (metacognition — the real prize):**
> "Last question, and it's the most important one: **the smartest thing I *thought* during this was…?**"
> *(thought — not did. Help her name a thinking move: "I saw the split was going to cause a fight before it did,"
> "I split the leftover into equal parts," "I made a rule even a sneaky person can't argue with.")*

**🎉 Close:** celebrate big — a star on the wall, a certificate, her choice. She built a real tool that
makes her *home* more peaceful *and* she got sharp at fractions. **That star is hers.**

**🚪 Open loop → into Term 3 (COMMUNITY):**
> "You've now caught problems in your **own brain** and in your **home.** Next time we point this detective
> brain **outside** — at a problem a neighbor or classmate already has. Start noticing: what's one thing
> near you that someone wishes worked better?"

**✍️ Log line:** *"The smartest thing I thought was ___."*
